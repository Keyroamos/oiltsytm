Part 2: Detailed Tarita OMC Business Processes 
1. Purpose of this document 
This document defines the business context, scope, and high-level structure of the Tarita Petroleum 
Management System (TPMS). It is based strictly on the information provided about Tarita's operations. 
Activities Tarita has not said it performs are deliberately excluded. Items that still need confirmation are 
collected in Section 10 so they can be resolved before design starts. 
2. Company and business model 
Tarita Petroleum Limited is an Oil Marketing Company operating in Kenya. The principal commercial 
activity relevant to TPMS is purchasing petroleum products from petroleum importers and suppliers, 
and selling those products to downstream petroleum retailers and customers. TPMS is therefore 
designed around Tarita's role as a wholesale petroleum buyer and seller.

3. System purpose 
TPMS gives Tarita a single, reliable record of its wholesale petroleum business, from purchase through to 
payment. It must let management answer the following questions at any time.

4. System scope 
4.1 In scope

What TPMS records 
A. Supplier management 
Suppliers and importers from whom Tarita buys petroleum products (for example, Gulf

B. Product management 
Petroleum products traded by Tarita. Initially PMS and AGO; the design must allow 
more products to be added later. 
C. Fuel procurement 
Purchases from suppliers: supplier, product, quantity, purchase price, and total cost. 
D. Fuel allocation 
Product, allocation period, allocated quantity, supplier or source, lifted quantity, and

E. Product movement 
Movements linked to Tarita's purchases and sales, keeping allocated, 
purchased, received, and sold quantities as separate records. Product 
movements must also retain the Local/Transit classification where applicable. 
F. Wholesale sales 
Sales to customers and retailers: customer, product, quantity, selling price, 
Local/Transit classification, eTIMS VAT tax amount, and total sales value. 
G. Customer 
management 
Name, contact information, KRA PIN, billing and delivery information, credit terms, 
account balance, and transaction history. 
H. Invoicing 
Invoices generated from wholesale sales. 
I. Payments 
Payments received from customers, and the resulting outstanding balance on each 
invoice. 
J. Purchase costing 
Purchase costs, including foreign-currency invoices, exchange rates, KES 
equivalents, import tax values, and taxes paid/associated with the specific 
product entry or receipt.

8. Tax, Product Entry and Local/Transit Classification 
Tax and market classification are core transaction attributes in TPMS. Tax information must remain linked to 
the specific product entry/receipt or sale to which it relates. Product transactions must also be capable of 
being classified as Local or Transit where applicable. 
8.1 Tax associated with product entry 
When petroleum product enters Tarita's records through a purchase, lifting, or receipt, TPMS shall capture 
the applicable tax paid or associated with that specific product entry. The tax shall not be treated only as a 
general expense detached from the product transaction. 
The product-entry record should be capable of identifying: 
• Product

• Quantity and unit

• Invoice value and currency

• Applicable tax type

• Tax reference or supporting document where applicable

8.2 Local and Transit classification 
TPMS shall distinguish petroleum product transactions classified as Local from those classified as Transit. 
This classification shall be available at the appropriate product movement, sales, stock and reporting levels, 
so that Tarita can separately identify local and transit quantities and values. 
The system shall therefore be able to answer: 
• How much of each product is classified as Local? 
• How much of each product is classified as Transit? 
• What quantity and value was sold locally? 
• What quantity and value was sold as Transit? 
• What tax was associated with the relevant product entry or sale? 
• What is the Local/Transit position for reporting purposes? 
8.3 Design rule 
Local/Transit classification and tax information must remain traceable to the underlying transaction. They 
must not be maintained only as summary figures on management reports.
